Reference architecture

Start here

From a distance, Democratic Services can look like agendas, minutes and finding a room at 10.30 on Tuesday. The deeper job is more consequential: it is the institutional machinery that helps a public body move from lawful authority and evidence to a transparent decision, an accurate record, action and durable institutional memory. Meetings are simply where some of that machinery becomes visible.

Make the right decision possible. Make the decision understandable. Make the record trustworthy. Make the next action unavoidable.

What this site is for

This is a public, employer-neutral handbook and laboratory: a place to examine how the machinery works before reaching for a grand transformation programme. It describes a reference architecture that can be localised to a real authority without confusing national law with local constitutional arrangements. The bias is deliberate: prefer boring, reversible improvements that produce evidence over impressive-looking change that can create another dependency.

Start with one irritation

Do not begin by “implementing AI”. Begin with a genuine problem: a paper that is difficult to check, a committee whose history is hard to reconstruct, a recurring deadline that lives in somebody's memory, or a governance question whose source chain is unclear. The unit of progress is not novelty; it is a problem made measurably less troublesome. Improve that one thing, measure it, and keep or discard the method based on evidence.

Working legacy system?
An awkward spreadsheet can be a surprisingly sophisticated institution. A colour may signal a clearance, an extra tab may preserve a recurring deadline, and a formula may encode years of remembered practice. If the current service works through duplicated spreadsheets, informal colour conventions, overloaded mailboxes, personal filing habits or expert memory, do not begin by sweeping them away. First learn what each workaround is doing. Use Quiet modernisation and Mailbox & Outlook modernisation.

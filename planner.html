Planning reference

Build the plan from evidence, not theatre

A useful plan is less a declaration of intent than a chain of questions that survives contact with reality. The planning workbench maps those questions, the evidence they need and the specialist combinations that can answer them. It helps a locally authorised Copilot session reason across the handbook while keeping the public website firmly on the reference side of the trust boundary.

The planning spine

Seven questions before a plan deserves a name

The questions are intentionally unglamorous. That is useful: most weak plans fail because they skipped something ordinary — authority, ownership, an actual capability, or the awkward control hidden inside the old process.

Reconstruct the current process, artefacts, owners, dependencies, workarounds and pain points before suggesting replacement.

Temporary coalitions

The engines flock according to the problem

Ten engines are enough if they can combine intelligently. Coalitions are temporary combinations of the existing ten engines: a way to bring the right questions together without inventing a new department every time the problem changes its hat. They do not create new authority, new specialist identities or a new platform.

Archaeology coalition

Understand a legacy process

01 + 02 + 09 + 10

Context Builder reconstructs what exists; Research tests external claims; Data & Performance exposes structure and duplication; Challenge identifies the control that must survive.

Useful for: inherited spreadsheets, trackers, mailbox habits, manual registers and mysterious recurring chores.

Committee-cycle coalition

Make an upcoming cycle boringly reliable

03 + 04 + 05 + 07 + 08

Governance establishes the route; Committee Intelligence reconstructs the body and cycle; Paper Readiness checks the decision instrument; Workflow builds the backwards plan; Drafting produces clear requests and instructions.

Useful for: committee deadlines, agendas, reports, forward work and senior chasing.

Decision-integrity coalition

Stress-test a proposed decision

02 + 03 + 05 + 10

Evidence, authority, paper readiness and challenge are brought together before the decision route is treated as safe.

Useful for: delegations, reports, recommendations, legal/governance ambiguity and assurance.

Microsoft reality coalition

Separate a tool myth from a usable capability

01 + 02 + 09 + 10

The current artefact is inspected, the vendor claim is verified, the actual environment is tested and the smallest reversible improvement is challenged.

Useful for: Power Query, workbook links, Lists, OneNote, Outlook, shared mailboxes and automation claims.

Meeting-to-memory coalition

Protect the record after the room empties

04 + 05 + 06 + 07

The body, decision route, formal record and follow-through are kept connected so actions and institutional memory do not evaporate after the meeting.

Useful for: minutes, decision notices, actions, approvals and follow-up.

Communication-under-pressure coalition

Get a deadline unstuck without drama

05 + 07 + 08 + 10

Readiness identifies the missing thing, Workflow calculates the real timing, Drafting makes the ask clear, and Challenge removes invented urgency or unnecessary escalation.

Useful for: late papers, senior reminders, exact deadlines and defensible escalation.

Plan shape

A useful plan fits on one screen before it becomes a programme

If the outcome, present reality, authority, capability, owner and evidence cannot be stated compactly, adding more pages will not make the uncertainty disappear. Start with the bounded shape; expand only when the work earns it.

Outcome

One sentence describing the improvement in operational terms.

Baseline

What happens today, including the control hidden inside awkward practice.

Authority

The source chain and the professional boundary.

Capability

Only the Microsoft/tool features verified for the exact use.

Baby step

The smallest reversible change capable of producing useful evidence.

Owner & timing

Who does what, by when, and why that date exists.

Evidence

What result would support keeping the change—and what would falsify it.

Stop / refer

The condition requiring human approval, competent advice or no further work.

Progressive route

Broad modernisation still moves one proof at a time

Large change is simply accumulated evidence when it is done well. Observe something real, frame it precisely, test the smallest useful move, and let the result decide whether the next step deserves to exist.

Observepublic baseline + permitted local archaeology
Frameone problem and the control it performs
Verifyauthority, evidence and actual capability
Testone reversible improvement
Evidenceresult, defect, rollback and learning
Standardiseplain-English method only after proof
Continue or stopnext bounded step, professional referral or closure

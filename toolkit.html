Registers, templates and prompt patterns

Operating toolkit

The toolkit should be smaller than the service. Every register, template and prompt must earn its maintenance cost.

The eight registers are a target information model, not an instruction to create eight new trackers. First map the existing spreadsheets, systems and lists. A current workbook may legitimately contain several of these concepts; several workbooks may be duplicate views of one concept. Consolidate only after ownership, dependencies and the authoritative record are understood.

ROUTE example

GOAL: Determine the likely decision route for this proposal.
CONTEXT: English local-government Democratic Services.
SOURCES: Attached constitution extracts and report.
LIMITS: Do not infer delegation absent from sources. Separate law from local procedure.
OUTPUT: Route, evidence chain and unresolved questions.
VERIFY / STOP: Cite each authority step and stop where competent interpretation is required.

Legacy spreadsheet triage

Before creating a replacement, classify each significant workbook:

ClassMeaning
KEEPExcel is still the right tool; document owner and improve hygiene.
REPAIRPurpose is valid but validation, naming, structure or protection needs improvement.
REPLACEThe workbook is acting as a poor shared database/workflow and another existing tool is materially better.
RETIREDuplicate, obsolete or superseded — archive/read-only first where needed, then remove from active use.

Decision readiness comes first

A perfectly administered meeting can still consider a poor paper. Ask “is this actually decision-ready?” before celebrating efficient publication.

For cultural adoption and low-confidence users, use the Quiet modernisation method.

Independent and unofficial. This is a reference model, not employer procedure, legal advice or official governance guidance. Verify current law, authority type, current constitution and competent professional advice before relying on a proposition.

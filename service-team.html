Module 4 · Build a resilient service

Build the service, not just the rota

A successful Democratic Services team is not defined by a fashionable structure or a particular headcount. It is defined by whether the organisation has the capabilities, ownership, cover, professional judgement and learning needed to support democratic decision-making reliably.

Why it matters

A process can look controlled while depending on one person's memory. A committee cycle can meet every deadline while staff are permanently overloaded. A new system can reduce clicks while making nobody responsible for the final record. Service design therefore has to include people, capacity, knowledge and quality—not merely workflow diagrams.

What it is: capability before job title

Start with the twelve service capabilities in the Operating Model. Then ask who owns each capability, who provides cover, what competence is required, where specialist advice comes from and how the work is checked. In a small authority one person may legitimately cover several capabilities. In a larger service they may be distributed across teams. The public reference should not invent a universal staffing ratio.

Authority and professional context

The current Best Value statutory guidance for England, issued 8 May 2024, treats continuous improvement, leadership, governance, culture, use of resources, service delivery and partnerships/community engagement as connected. It specifically identifies capacity and capability, succession planning, member/officer development, constructive relationships and learning from failure as characteristics relevant to a well-functioning authority. A consultation on revised Best Value statutory guidance opened on 15 July 2026; that consultation is a currentness watch, not a replacement for the operative 2024 guidance at this review date.

ADSO's professional learning offer spans committee services, scrutiny, civic teams, member services and community governance. LGA guidance on councillor induction and development also treats effective member support as an organised learning function rather than a one-off information dump. CIPFA/Solace's governance framework and 2025 addendum provide a wider professional benchmark for reviewing whether governance arrangements remain fit for purpose.

This is not HR policy. Actual structures, grades, job descriptions, recruitment, performance management, employment terms, equality duties, union arrangements and workforce policies belong to the real organisation and applicable law. This page teaches service-design questions, not managerial authority.

Worked example: a four-person service

Scenario. A fictional authority has four people in Democratic Services. Their titles are irrelevant to the exercise. The manager says, “Everyone helps with everything.”

That may be collegial, but it does not yet prove resilience. Map the capabilities instead:

The question is not whether every box has a different person. It is whether a predictable absence, vacancy or peak workload makes an essential capability disappear.

You try: reconstruct the team risk

Choose one service capability and answer:

  1. Who is the primary owner?
  2. Who can cover it tomorrow without relying on undocumented memory?
  3. What artefact or source lets them reconstruct current state?
  4. What competence must not be faked?
  5. Which other professional function must be involved?
  6. What is the observable workload signal?
  7. How would a new starter learn it?
  8. How is quality checked?

If several answers are “only Jane knows”, that is useful evidence about the service—not criticism of Jane.

What good practice notices

Clear ownership

Every recurring outcome has someone accountable for getting it through the next meaningful state.

Real cover

Cover means another competent person can reconstruct the work, not merely access the same mailbox.

Induction

New starters and members need staged learning linked to real cycles and responsibilities.

Quality assurance

Checks concentrate on decision-critical risk, not ceremonial sign-off.

Capacity evidence

Use workload, delay, rework and missed dependencies rather than “we are busy” as the only signal.

Learning culture

Near misses and awkward exceptions should improve the method rather than disappear into private memory.

Localise it

Map the real service against the twelve capabilities. Add current owners, cover, specialist dependencies, workload evidence, critical knowledge locations and development needs. Then compare this with the organisation's authorised structure, job descriptions, workforce policies and management arrangements. Where those sources conflict with the public reference, the local authoritative arrangement governs.

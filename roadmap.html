One small, reversible improvement at a time

Baby-step roadmap

Modernise in increments small enough to reverse, test and explain — and small enough that experienced staff do not have to abandon a working mental model before the replacement has proved itself.

Migration rule: observe the workaround → identify the control it performs → stabilise it → structure obvious data → remove duplicate capture → migrate only where the new home is demonstrably better.

Read Quiet modernisation.

0 — Public research

Build the source model. No workplace change.

1 — Personal learning

Establish the operator baseline: understand duties, bodies, decision routes, recurring business, cycles, professional interfaces and the real Microsoft capability available. Use only information/tools permitted by local policy. Keep a quiet evidence trail of reliable outcomes and learning.

2 — Historical public-document testing

Test Context Builder, Research and Paper Readiness against published material with known outcomes.

3 — Governance intelligence

Add Governance Localiser and Committee Intelligence; reconstruct selected authorities from public sources.

4 — Personal low-risk productivity

Where policy allows: public research, drafting from non-sensitive facts, deadline models and published-paper QA.

5 — One recurring process

Choose one irritation staff already recognise — for example duplicated updating, inconsistent statuses, paper readiness or backwards planning. Improve that single process in a reversible pilot.

Include at least one person who likes the change and, where possible, one experienced sceptic. The enthusiastic user tests usefulness; the sceptic is often better at discovering what the redesign forgot.

6 — Team-visible optional method

Share the useful checklist or structured method. It should still work for colleagues who do not want to use AI.

Support adoption through task-based micro-coaching and respected peer champions rather than generic “digital transformation” training. Microsoft recommends champion networks for Office 365 adoption; NHS improvement guidance similarly emphasises shared purpose, involvement, measurement and small tests of change.

7 — Shared knowledge

Only after value is demonstrated should shared Lists, SharePoint IA or team structures change.

8 — Controlled automation

Only after the relevant capability is verified and the manual/deterministic process is stable: automate suitable steps such as reminders, status changes, recurring-date calculations or notifications. Make ownership, failure and rollback visible. No automation platform is assumed or required.

9 — Broader operating model

Only after multiple small improvements demonstrate value should the wider model be reconsidered.

Transferable practice
The GOV.UK Service Standard emphasises user needs, whole problems, simplicity, iteration, privacy, success measures and suitable technology. Those disciplines transfer without pretending Democratic Services is itself a GOV.UK transaction.

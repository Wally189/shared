Why time matters in 2026

Law / mandatory The English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026. Its provisions do not all commence in the same way. Section 108 governs commencement, while section 102 preserves specified pre-existing subordinate legislation. A current-law engine therefore asks “in force when, for whom, and subject to what saved instrument?” rather than merely finding the newest Act.

Core source families

  • Local Government Act 1972, including Part VA access-to-information provisions where applicable. The latest England text of section 100B currently uses five clear days for material within that provision; do not generalise the rule without localisation.
  • Local Government Act 2000.
  • Localism Act 2011.
  • Local Government and Housing Act 1989.
  • Combined Authorities (Overview and Scrutiny Committees, Access to Information and Audit Committees) Order 2017, read with current amendments and authority-specific instruments.
  • Local Authorities (Executive Arrangements) (Meetings and Access to Information) (England) Regulations 2012 where applicable.
  • Openness of Local Government Bodies Regulations 2014.
  • Freedom of Information Act 2000 and ICO section 46 records guidance.
  • Equality Act 2010, section 149.
  • Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, read with commencement instruments and the wider current data-protection framework.

Scrutiny baseline

Statutory guidance The government's 2024 overview-and-scrutiny guidance applies to councils, combined authorities and combined county authorities in England. It covers culture, resourcing, membership, access to information, work planning and evidence sessions, while recognising that local arrangements differ.

Reference architecture

Start here

Democratic Services is not fundamentally a meeting-arrangements function. It is the institutional machinery that helps a public body move from lawful authority and evidence to a transparent decision, an accurate record, action and durable institutional memory.

Make the right decision possible. Make the decision understandable. Make the record trustworthy. Make the next action unavoidable.

The five-layer model

1. Authority

What may happen, under which law, constitution, delegation and procedure.

2. Knowledge

What the organisation knows about bodies, powers, sources, precedents, recurring business and history.

3. Decision flow

How proposals become properly evidenced, routed, considered, decided, recorded and followed through.

4. Workbench

Ordinary Microsoft 365, structured information, deterministic tools and bounded AI engines.

5. Assurance & improvement

Verification, measurement, testing, learning and controlled incremental change.

What this site is for

This is a public, employer-neutral handbook and laboratory. It describes a reference architecture that can be localised to a real authority without confusing national law with local constitutional arrangements. It deliberately prefers boring, reversible improvements to large transformation programmes.

  1. Read the relevant method.
  2. Use the appropriate source or engine instruction.
  3. Work only with information your organisation permits you to use.
  4. Verify the result against authoritative material.
  5. Put the final record in the organisation's actual authoritative system.

Start with one irritation

Do not begin by “implementing AI”. Begin with a genuine problem: a paper that is difficult to check, a committee whose history is hard to reconstruct, a recurring deadline that lives in somebody's memory, or a governance question whose source chain is unclear. Improve that one thing, measure it, and keep or discard the method based on evidence.

If the starting point is a functioning but messy legacy system—duplicate spreadsheets, colour-coded conventions, weak filenames and colleagues who know the process better than the documentation—start with Quiet modernisation. The first job is to understand what the mess is doing before tidying it.

Public-safe by design
Nothing on this site should depend on obscurity for protection. If material would be awkward if read tomorrow by a chief executive, Monitoring Officer, councillor, journalist, union representative or software provider, it does not belong here.

Independent and unofficial. This is a reference model, not employer procedure, legal advice or official governance guidance. Verify current law, authority type, current constitution and competent professional advice before relying on a proposition.

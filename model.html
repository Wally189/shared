Decision integrity, not meeting administration

What Democratic Services is for

The useful unit is not “a meeting”. It is a decision journey: authority → evidence → route → consideration → decision → record → action → memory. Democratic Services helps that journey remain lawful, intelligible, timely and retrievable while preserving the authority of elected members and specialist officers.

By the end

You should be able to explain why Democratic Services exists, name the capabilities a reliable function needs, distinguish political decision-making from officer and professional responsibilities, and recognise why local rules must be checked before a generic model becomes operational advice.

Three kinds of responsibility

Democratic leadership

Elected members exercise the political and democratic roles given to them by law and local governance arrangements. Democratic Services supports the process; it does not make political choices for them.

Officer administration

Officers prepare, advise, administer and implement within their roles and delegations. The exact division of responsibility varies locally.

Professional advice

Monitoring Officer/Legal, Finance/S151, information governance and other specialists retain their own professional responsibilities. Democratic Services must know when their judgement or assurance is required rather than silently absorbing it.

Practice note: the twelve-capability model is this site's synthesis, not a statutory staffing specification. Current LGA material describes Democratic Services as supporting formal decision-making and linking elected members, senior officers and the public; LGA development guidance also illustrates Democratic Services' role in committee and chair development. Actual structures and job responsibilities remain local.

Three truth layers

National baseline

Applicable legislation, secondary legislation, statutory guidance, information law, accessibility requirements and relevant standards.

Local governance

The body's enabling instruments, constitution, standing orders, terms of reference, schemes of delegation and current local procedures.

Live case

The particular paper, meeting, decision, action or governance question.

Never skip the middle layer. “The legislation generally provides…” does not become “therefore this committee has power to…” until authority type, commencement, local instruments and delegation have been checked.

Professional boundaries

Knowing when another professional must answer is itself a governance capability. Democratic Services should make dependencies visible and protect the route to competent advice; it should not manufacture a clearance, legal interpretation or financial assurance.

Check your understanding

A report author says, “Legal looked at something similar last year, so this one is cleared.” What can you establish from that statement? What remains unknown? Which local evidence or professional owner would settle it? If your answer begins by inventing the clearance, go back one square.

Independent and unofficial. Verify current law, authority type, current constitution and competent professional advice before relying on a proposition.

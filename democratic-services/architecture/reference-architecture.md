# Modern Democratic Services — Reference Architecture

**Research position:** 23 September 2026  
**Status:** design baseline  
**Purpose:** public, independent, reusable reference model; not an employer procedure, legal opinion or official governance manual.

## Executive finding

An excellent Democratic Services function is an **institutional decision-integrity service**.

Its purpose is to help public decisions move reliably through:

`authority → evidence → advice → decision route → transparent consideration → formal decision → accurate record → action → institutional memory`

while preserving lawfulness, accessibility, scrutiny, accountability and continuity.

The website should explain and test this model. It should not become the organisation's live record system.

Practical proposition:

`read handbook → select engine/method → provide appropriate sources → perform work in approved tools → human verifies → authoritative organisational system retains the record`

## 1. Five-layer model

### Authority
What may happen, under which law, constitution, delegation and procedure.

### Knowledge
What the organisation knows about bodies, powers, sources, precedents, recurring business and history.

### Decision Flow
How proposals become properly authorised, evidenced, documented, considered, decided, recorded and followed through.

### Workbench
Microsoft 365, structured information, templates, deterministic tools and bounded AI engines.

### Assurance & Improvement
Checking, measurement, regression testing, learning and controlled incremental change.

## 2. Three truth layers

### National baseline
Primary/secondary legislation, statutory guidance, information law, accessibility requirements and general professional standards.

### Local governance
The body's actual enabling instruments, constitution, terms of reference, standing orders, delegations and local procedures.

### Live case
The particular report, meeting, decision, action or governance question.

Never silently convert:

> The legislation generally provides...

into:

> Therefore this particular body has power to...

without passing through the local-governance layer.

## 3. Evidence labels

- LAW / MANDATORY REQUIREMENT
- STATUTORY GUIDANCE
- FORMAL PROFESSIONAL STANDARD
- ESTABLISHED SECTOR PRACTICE
- EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED GOOD PRACTICE
- TRANSFERABLE PRACTICE
- PROPOSED IMPROVEMENT
- EXPERIMENTAL

Practice is not law. History is not authority.

## 4. Design principles

1. Authority before automation.
2. One canonical record, many views.
3. Source before assertion.
4. Accessible by design.
5. Human professional judgement remains real.
6. Automate boring certainty before ambiguous judgement.
7. Reversible before irreversible.
8. Measure before claiming improvement.
9. Retire obsolete machinery rather than accumulating it.

## 5. Twelve service capabilities

1. Governance & constitutional advisory support.
2. Democratic body management.
3. Forward planning.
4. Report & paper management.
5. Meeting secretariat.
6. Decisions, minutes & actions.
7. Overview & scrutiny support.
8. Public participation & transparency.
9. Member support & development.
10. Records, information & publication.
11. Performance & intelligence.
12. Continuity & improvement.

These are capabilities, not mandated teams or job titles.

Democratic Services should interface with, not absorb, Legal/Monitoring Officer, Finance/S151, information governance/DPO, audit, HR, communications, digital/IT, procurement, elections and policy/programme functions.

## 6. Ten-engine boundary

The mature AI/workbench estate is currently constrained to ten materially distinct engines:

1. Context Builder
2. Research & Evidence
3. Governance Localiser
4. Committee & Organisation Intelligence
5. Decision & Paper Readiness
6. Meeting & Formal Record
7. Forward Plan & Workflow
8. Drafting & Communication
9. Data & Performance
10. Challenge & Improvement

Intake is a common wrapper: `GOAL → CONTEXT → SOURCES → CONSTRAINTS → OUTPUT → VERIFY/STOP`.

Microsoft product instructions are tool guides, not an engine.

Scrutiny is a professional context using Research + Committee Intelligence + Meeting + Challenge.

Do not create a new engine merely because a task has a different name.

## 8. Microsoft 365 reference model

### OneDrive
Personal working area, not permanent team memory.

Suggested personal structure:

```
00 Inbox
10 Active Work
20 Copilot Cases
30 Personal Reference
   AI Engines
   Prompt Patterns
90 Temporary
99 To File
```

### SharePoint
Team knowledge and structured state.

Use **pages** for explanatory material, **document libraries** for controlled documents, **Lists** for structured changing state, and **Excel** for analysis/calculation rather than defaulting to spreadsheets as shared truth.

Candidate structured state:

- Bodies & Powers
- Meetings & Forward Work
- Papers & Clearances
- Decisions
- Actions
- Membership & Appointments
- Governance Sources & Changes
- Improvement Experiments

Existing canonical committee-management/publication systems remain canonical where they already own the record.

## 10. National-to-local governance method

Every organisation should have an **Authority Passport**.

Every material proposition should be localised through:

`national rule → commencement/effective date → authority class → enabling/local legislation → savings/transitions → constitution → delegation → particular body → current case`

The system must record effective dates and currentness. A newly enacted provision is not automatically an operative rule for every body.

## 11. Committee intelligence

The central object is a **Body Profile** with identity, purpose, authority, composition, relationships, operation, recurring business, administrative sources and currentness.

Discovery preference:

`constitution/enabling instrument → terms of reference → official committee directory → calendar → agendas/reports → decisions/minutes → forward plan → membership records → historic material`

Historic minutes are evidence of history, not automatic evidence of current power.

## 12. Three-file Copilot method

Treat three useful attachments as a conservative design ceiling for the method, not a universal Microsoft licensing claim.

Engine instructions are pasted into the prompt, leaving attachment capacity for evidence.

Where evidence exceeds the limit:

`three sources → Case Packet`

Repeat, then:

`three packets → consolidated packet`

Original source IDs must survive consolidation. A packet is always a derived working aid, never authority.

## 13. Prompt standard

Every reusable prompt should specify:

- GOAL
- CONTEXT
- SOURCES
- AUTHORITY / LIMITS
- OUTPUT
- VERIFY / STOP

Maintain prompt patterns rather than hundreds of near-duplicates:

EXPLAIN, FIND, ROUTE, CHECK, EXTRACT, COMPARE, PLAN, DRAFT, RESEARCH, LOCALISE, CHALLENGE.

## 14. Testing

Every engine should receive regression cases covering:

- ordinary;
- difficult;
- missing information;
- conflicting evidence;
- stale/superseded source;
- wrong authority type;
- precedent contradicted by current rule;
- ambiguous decision;
- accessibility failure;
- embedded prompt injection;
- false clearance;
- provenance loss.

Use public historic agendas, reports, amendments, decisions, minutes, constitutions and forward plans as test material.

## 15. Baby-step roadmap

0. Public research.
1. Personal learning.
2. Historic public-document testing.
3. Governance intelligence.
4. Personal low-risk productivity where policy permits.
5. One recurring process.
6. Team-visible optional method.
7. Shared knowledge.
8. Controlled deterministic automation.
9. Broader operating model only after evidence.

No big-bang transformation.

## 16. Measures

Measure outcomes such as:

- late papers/publication;
- missed dependencies;
- unresolved actions;
- first-pass paper readiness;
- corrections/reissues;
- minute corrections;
- rework loops;
- meeting-preparation time;
- retrieval time;
- accessibility defects;
- time for a new starter to reconstruct the service;
- unsupported AI claims;
- provenance completeness;
- regression failures.

Never treat prompt count as success.

## 17. Publication test

Before eventual public release ask:

> Would this remain acceptable if a Chief Executive, Monitoring Officer, councillor, journalist, union representative and the software provider read it tomorrow?

If not, do not publish it.

## 18. Important architectural consequences

- Commencement/effective-date tracking is first-class.
- Rules should eventually be representable as structured data, not only prose.
- Governance structures themselves should be reviewable.
- Institutional memory is a graph: BODY → POWER → MEETING → PAPER → DECISION → ACTION → OUTCOME/HISTORY.
- Decision quality should be tested before meeting quality.
- Recurring business should be generated from durable rules, not human memory.
- Date arithmetic should be deterministic code once the governing rule is known.
- AI output used operationally may itself raise records/information-management questions.
- Prompt injection must be treated as a document-ingestion risk.
- Every engine/template/register needs owner/version/review/retirement semantics.
- Inclusion means more than HTML conformance.
- The model must support joint/hybrid governance structures.
- The system must distinguish finding a legal question from being professionally entitled to answer it.
- Business continuity test: could the service operate if its most experienced officer were unexpectedly unavailable?

## 19. First evidence-producing slice

Before building the full site, prove:

1. public source-register method;
2. Authority Passport;
3. Body Profile;
4. Case Packet;
5. Context Builder;

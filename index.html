Learn the profession before choosing the tools

Understand, run and improve Democratic Services

Democratic Services is the professional machinery that helps a public body move from lawful authority and evidence to a transparent decision, an accurate record, action and durable institutional memory. Meetings are where some of that machinery becomes visible; they are not the whole job.

Make the right decision possible. Make the decision understandable. Make the record trustworthy. Make the next action unavoidable.

Golden path

New to Democratic Services?

Follow this route in order. It begins with the profession, follows one decision from beginning to end, then moves into service design and improvement. Experienced practitioners can jump straight to the professional reference below.

  1. Understand the job. Learn the purpose, capabilities and professional boundaries. Start with the operating model.
  2. Follow one decision. See how a realistic proposal moves through authority, advice, papers, a meeting, a formal record and follow-up. Follow the worked journey.
  3. Understand the rules. Separate national law from the body's own constitution, delegation and procedure. Learn the authority model.
  4. Understand the service. See the people, capacity, handovers, quality and relationships needed to keep the machinery reliable. Run a reliable service.
  5. Discover current reality. Investigate the real organisation before proposing change. Use Local Organisation Discovery.
  6. Diagnose. Find hidden controls, friction, risk and maturity rather than guessing. Baseline capability.
  7. Improve one thing safely. Preserve the useful control, test a small change and measure the result. Use Quiet modernisation.
  8. Choose tools only if needed. Then consider Microsoft 365, tool diagnosis or bounded AI.
  9. Verify and learn. Test the outcome and keep, change, roll back or retire it. Use the improvement laboratory.

The five-layer model

1. Authority

What may happen, under which law, constitution, delegation and procedure.

2. Knowledge

What the organisation knows about bodies, powers, sources, precedents, recurring business and history.

3. Decision flow

How proposals become properly evidenced, routed, considered, decided, recorded and followed through.

4. Workbench

Ordinary tools and structured information chosen for an understood professional problem.

5. Assurance & improvement

Verification, measurement, testing, learning and controlled incremental change.

Learn Democratic Services

Use this route if you want to understand the profession before reaching for the tools. It begins with local democracy and professional roles, follows one decision from proposal to durable record, then moves into the service as a system: people, information, controls, improvement and only then technology.

Already experienced?
You do not need to work through every stage. Use the reference routes directly. The Golden Path exists so that a newcomer never has to guess what ought to come before what.

What you should be able to do

  • Explain why Democratic Services exists and where its authority stops.
  • Trace a proposal through the democratic decision lifecycle without confusing national law, local rules and the live case.
  • Understand the core professional craft: bodies, papers, meetings, formal records, forward work, scrutiny, member support and transparency.
  • Reconstruct an inherited service before redesigning it.
  • Map the people, capabilities, information and controls needed for a resilient service.
  • Choose one bounded improvement, measure it and keep, change or reverse it on evidence.
  • Use Microsoft 365 and AI only when they solve an evidenced problem within actual organisational authority.

The Golden Path

  1. Enter the profession. Foundations of Democratic Services — local democracy, professional purpose, members and officers, impartiality and boundaries.
  2. Follow one decision. A democratic decision, end to end — proposal, authority, advice, route, paper, publication, meeting, record and follow-through.
  3. See the whole service. The operating model — twelve capabilities and the three truth layers.
  4. Understand the people system. Build the service, not just the rota — capability, cover, induction, quality, resilience and learning.
  5. Localise reality. Local Organisation Discovery — reconstruct the actual organisation, process and hidden controls.
  6. Assess current capability. Maturity & measurement — evidence, outcomes and the next material gap.
  7. Improve carefully. Quiet modernisation and Baby-step roadmap — change one real problem without declaring war on the people keeping the service alive.
  8. Use tools proportionately. Microsoft 365, tool diagnosis and AI workbench.
  9. Assure and learn. Planning workbench and Improvement laboratory — define evidence before claiming improvement.

How each teaching module works

Why it matters

Begin with the professional problem, not the vocabulary.

What it is

Explain the concept before abstracting it into a framework.

Authority

Distinguish law, local constitution, guidance, professional practice and interpretation.

Worked example

Follow a realistic fictional case and notice the judgement points.

You try

Apply the method rather than merely agreeing with the prose.

Localise and continue

Identify what must be checked locally and where the next lesson starts.

One rule throughout

Learn the profession first. Diagnose the real service second. Change the system third. Technology comes after the problem is understood.
